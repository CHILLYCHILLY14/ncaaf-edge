How to read this. Edge is the model's probability minus the break-even probability of the price on offer — a 5% edge on a −110 line means the model thinks the bet wins 57.4% of the time where the price only needs 52.4%. Tiers are edge thresholds, scaled up when the model has thin data on a matchup, so an early-season number has to be bigger to earn the same label.
Nothing here is a guarantee. The model is one opinion priced against a market that is very good at its job. Check Model History before trusting a number — it tracks every prediction the model has ever made, not just the ones that became bets, which is the far larger and less biased sample. Bet only what you can afford to lose.
One book, always. ESPN's free feed never carries more than a single sportsbook's line per game — there's no line shopping available without a paid odds API, so every price shown is whatever that one book posted. A market is actionable only when ESPN supplies both real side prices; the model never fills a missing price with a placeholder.
